Understand the metal

10K, 14K, 18K, 22K and 24K gold

Karat describes the proportion of gold in an alloy. It is different from the carat weight used for diamonds. A stamp helps identify a piece, but plating, solder, repairs and mixed components can affect what is present.

An item-specific offer

How is a cash-for-gold offer determined?

For scrap gold, a useful starting point is the weight of the gold-bearing metal multiplied by its verified gold proportion and a current pure-gold price per unit of weight. This estimates metal value; it is not a guaranteed purchase price.

Spot gold is a market reference for gold itself. Jewelry's total weight may include stones, springs or other non-gold parts. Keep grams and troy ounces consistent when comparing calculations. Our gold valuation guide explains the distinction.

A purchase offer can differ from theoretical metal value because of recovery or refining costs, resale potential, market conditions and the buyer's margin. Designer provenance, collectible coin features and natural diamonds need separate consideration. Ask for the final net amount and the factors behind it.

  1. Identify and examine the items

    Markings, construction, maker details and condition help establish what is being evaluated. A photograph or receipt can assist but does not replace inspecting the piece.

  2. Check purity and weight

    Ask how purity is tested, which parts are included in the weight and whether stones or non-gold components affect the calculation. Mixed karats should not be treated as one assumed purity.

  3. Review the purchase terms

    Compare the offer, any deductions and payment arrangements. A retail replacement appraisal, an online asking price and a purchase offer serve different purposes.

What should you know about gold testing?

Evaluation may involve reading hallmarks, examining construction and choosing a testing method suited to the item. X-ray fluorescence (XRF) analyzes metal composition, but plating can affect the result. Acid or touchstone testing uses a sample or streak. Neither a marking nor one surface reading should be treated as the whole evaluation.

Ask which method will be used on your piece and whether it could leave a mark or require a sample. Discuss that first, especially for collectible coins, inherited jewelry or signed pieces. Call before visiting if you have testing concerns.

For background, GIA explains touchstone testing and how plating can affect XRF results.

Compare estimates carefully

A phone estimate can change after inspection

A preliminary phone or photo estimate uses details that have not yet been verified. The final in-person offer may be higher or lower after purity, weight, condition, authenticity, stones, maker details and other characteristics are checked. Market prices can also move before your visit.

Ask what assumptions an estimate uses, what remains to be checked and whether the amount is the final net purchase offer. There is no obligation to sell simply because you request an evaluation.

What should you bring when selling gold?

  • The complete jewelry, coins or collection you want evaluated.

Questions about selling gold in Los Angeles

Can I sell broken jewelry or scrap gold?

Yes. We buy broken gold chains, damaged jewelry, single earrings and mixed scrap gold. An offer depends on the verified metal content and other characteristics of the actual items.

Does 18K gold always receive a higher offer than 14K?

18K has a higher nominal gold proportion per gram, but the offer also depends on weight, verified purity, stones, maker, condition and other features. A lighter 18K piece need not be worth more than a heavier 14K piece.

Do you buy gold coins and bullion?

We buy gold coins, including collections. Keep their holders and paperwork. Call ahead about bars or other bullion to confirm the format and quantity can be evaluated.

Can I sell inherited or designer gold jewelry?

Bring inherited and signed jewelry intact with available records. Discuss ownership authority when relevant. Maker, provenance, condition and stones may matter beyond gold content.

Will I receive the spot price for my jewelry?

Spot price is a reference for gold, not a guaranteed jewelry purchase price. Actual metal weight, purity, costs and other resale characteristics affect the offer. Ask for an explanation of the final net amount.

Why might my phone estimate differ from the final offer?

A phone estimate relies on unverified details. Purity, weight, authenticity, condition and other characteristics are checked in person; market conditions may also change. Compare the final offer after those checks.

Should I remove diamonds before selling gold jewelry?

Bring the complete piece first. Removing stones may damage jewelry or discard useful information. Our diamond buying covers natural, cut and polished diamonds; ask how the gold and eligible stones are considered together.
