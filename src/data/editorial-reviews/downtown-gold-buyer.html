Ask for the weight, purity, and offer basis

A useful comparison identifies the same pieces in each offer. For a mixed lot, ask whether 10K, 14K, 18K, and other purities are recorded separately. Confirm the weight unit and how diamonds, springs, or other components affect the metal weight used.

Karat describes the proportion of gold in an alloy: 14K means 14 parts out of 24. Gold plating and gold-filled construction describe different materials. The FTC guide to jewelry markings explains these distinctions. A visible stamp is a starting point for an examination, rather than a substitute for testing the item.

  • Items included: Are both quotes for the complete lot or only selected pieces?
  • Measured weight: Are the units and treatment of non-gold components clear?
  • Tested purity: Is the buyer explaining different karats and any uncertain components?
  • Net payment: What is the final amount after any stated charges, and how will it be paid?

Give jewelry and coins the appropriate evaluation

A broken unbranded chain, a diamond-set Cuban bracelet, and a collectible gold coin need different discussions. Ask whether an item is being considered for its metal, its stones, or resale as a complete piece. Avoid dismantling jewelry or removing diamonds before that discussion.

Bring existing diamond reports, maker documentation, and coin inventory notes with the matching items. Those records help identify what you have; they do not guarantee a premium. Keep potentially collectible coins in their holders and mention any known history.

The World Gold Council's purity and fineness guide is useful when interpreting karat and fineness markings. Our coin-buyer guide covers questions specific to coin collections.

Choose a selling process you can evaluate

An in-person visit lets you ask questions while the buyer examines your property. A mail-in service involves shipping, receipt, an evaluation period, and written acceptance or return terms. Before using either option, establish what happens if you decline an offer and when your property or payment will be returned.

An outright sale also differs from a pawn loan: a loan has repayment terms and the item serves as security. Compare the transaction you actually want. The business category alone does not establish which buyer will make the better offer.

Questions before your first visit

Is a purchase offer the same as an insurance appraisal?

No. A purchase offer states what a buyer is willing to pay. An insurance appraisal serves a different purpose and may use a different value basis. Say what you need before arranging an evaluation.

What if I only want to sell some of the items?

Identify those pieces before accepting. Ask for a revised amount for the selected items, because a quote for a full collection may not apply unchanged to a smaller group.
