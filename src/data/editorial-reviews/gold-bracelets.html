Why Cuban-link construction matters

Two bracelets of similar width can have different weights. Hollow links contain less metal than comparable solid links, while clasps and repairs may introduce components that need separate examination. Ask which parts are included in the gold calculation rather than treating the entire scale reading as pure gold.

For example, 14K describes an alloy containing 14 parts gold out of 24; it does not mean every gram of the bracelet is pure gold. A gold-plated or gold-filled item has a different construction from karat-gold jewelry. The FTC's explanation of gold markings and plating helps distinguish these terms.

Evaluate diamond-set bracelets as complete pieces

A diamond-set Cuban bracelet needs more than a gold-weight calculation. Ask how the stones are identified and whether their contribution is included in the offer. Total diamond weight across many small stones does not describe the size or quality of any individual diamond.

Diamond quality involves carat weight, color, clarity, and cut, as explained in GIA's diamond quality guide. Bring any existing reports, while recognizing that a report may cover a particular stone rather than every stone in a bracelet.

Do not remove stones yourself. Request an evaluation of the complete bracelet first, then clarify the terms of any proposed disassembly. Our diamond-buying service provides a starting point for discussing significant stones.

Broken, vintage, and signed bracelets need different attention

A broken clasp does not remove the gold already present in a bracelet. For a piece evaluated primarily for metal, tested purity and recoverable gold weight remain central. Damage can nevertheless affect the value of jewelry that might otherwise be resold intact.

Keep maker marks, original boxes, and useful history with a signed or antique bracelet. Do not assume an inherited piece should be sold only as scrap. Ask whether the design, condition, or maker gives the complete item a resale opportunity before authorizing repairs, polishing, or dismantling.

For a mixture of damaged jewelry, single earrings, and detached links, use our scrap-gold preparation guide. Keep separate pieces organized so you can follow the evaluation.

Compare the final amount for the same bracelets

Ask each buyer to identify the pieces, tested karats, weight units, treatment of stones, and net payment. A per-gram quote is only comparable when the material and calculation basis match. An attractive preliminary estimate is not the same as an offer following examination.

There is no single percentage of spot price that proves an offer is fair for every bracelet. A complete jewelry offer can reflect a different resale opportunity from a metal-only purchase. Request an explanation and compare the amount you would receive for the same items.

Should I repair a broken bracelet before selling?

Have it evaluated first. A repair may not add enough to the purchase offer to justify its cost, and a vintage or signed piece may need specialist attention. Bring detached components with the bracelet.

Can I sell a bracelet if I do not know its karat?

Yes, you can bring it for evaluation and explain what is unknown. The buyer needs to establish its material before making a meaningful offer; a photograph or color alone cannot confirm gold content.
